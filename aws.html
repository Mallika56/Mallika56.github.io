Chicago Divvy Lakehouse on AWS

Business problem

Divvy wants more annual members. Marketing needs to know when and where to reach casual riders, and the data team needs a pipeline that loads every monthly trip file reliably, flags bad records instead of silently dropping them, and keeps query costs low.

Approach

An AWS lakehouse defined in CloudFormation: monthly files land in an S3 raw zone, and a Glue PySpark job applies 11 data-quality rules. Rule-breaking rows go to a quarantine table with a reason code, and clean rows are written as Parquet partitioned by year and month, then queried in Athena with partition projection and a per-query scan cap. The same transformation code runs locally and is covered by 23 pytest tests and GitHub Actions CI.

Key findings

Members commute (8.4% of their weekday rides start in the 8am hour vs 4.4% for casual riders), while casual riders take longer leisure rides near the lakefront and museums, where 73–84% of starts are casual. The casual share swings from 42% in June to 17% in January. Fixing a Spark small-files issue cut the curated output from 2,368 files (7× the raw size) to 12 files (0.4× raw).

Data note: runs on a 1-in-256 random sample of the real Divvy trip files (Oct 2025 – Sep 2026, 24,019 rides). Percentages are sample estimates; the deploy script loads the full ~6M rides into AWS.

Why this one: it puts my AWS Data Engineer – Associate study into practice on my own city's data.
